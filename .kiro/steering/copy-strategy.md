## The Voice in One Sentence

**Quietly confident. Emotionally honest. Never tries to impress you — and that's exactly why it does.**

The reference points: Apple's compression and precision. Red Bull's identity-first storytelling. The emotional charge of something that actually matters.

---

## The Four Dials

| Dial | Setting |
|------|---------|
| Warmth | High — feels like a person, not a company |
| Authority | Medium-high — knows its stuff, doesn't need to prove it |
| Edge | Medium — has a point of view, not afraid to use it |
| Formality | Low-medium — conversational but never sloppy |

## What It Sounds Like

### Yes

- Short declarative sentences. Subject. Verb. Done.
- Fragments used intentionally for rhythm and emphasis.
- Second person always — "you", "your." Talking *to* the person, not *about* them.
- Present tense. Not "you will be able to" — "you can."
- Concrete over abstract. "RM 45/day" over "affordable pricing."
- Specificity as credibility. "GoPro Hero 13" not "action camera."
- Questions the reader is already asking themselves.
- The occasional one-liner that lands like a punch and needs nothing after it.

### Never

- Exclamation marks. Confidence doesn't shout.
- "We're passionate about..." — nobody cares.
- "Seamless experience" — meaningless filler.
- "World-class" / "best-in-class" / "cutting-edge" — empty superlatives.
- Passive voice. "The deposit is returned" → "We return your deposit."
- Explaining the joke. If you have to explain why something is cool, it isn't.
- Hedging. "We try to..." / "We aim to..." — either you do it or you don't.
- Corporate we. "Our team is dedicated to..." — no. Just do the thing.

### The rhythm rule

Every section needs at least one line that could stand alone as a poster. One line that stops the scroll. Everything else supports it.

## Tone by Section

Different sections serve different emotional jobs. The tone shifts to match.

| Section | Emotional job | Tone |
|---------|--------------|------|
| Hero | Arrest attention. Create desire. | Bold, declarative, slightly provocative |
| Gear / Product | Convert intent to action | Functional, precise, confident |
| Why Us / Value props | Remove doubt. Build trust. | Direct, honest, a little disarming |
| How It Works | Reduce friction | Clean, minimal, reassuring |
| Social Proof | Validate the decision | Warm, specific, human |
| FAQ | Handle objections | Straight-talking, no spin |
| Brand Statement / Close | Land the emotional close | Cinematic, charged, philosophical |

## The Emotional Architecture

The page tells a story. The user never reads it as a narrative, but the emotional journey is deliberate.

```
DESIRE     → Hero:           "I want that."
RELEVANCE  → Gear:           "That's exactly what I need."
PERMISSION → Why Us:         "It's okay to rent. It makes sense."
CONFIDENCE → How It Works:   "I know what to do."
VALIDATION → Social Proof:   "Other people like me did this."
RESOLUTION → FAQ:            "My last objection is answered."
IDENTITY   → Brand Close:    "This is who I am."
```

The final section is the most important and the most underused. It's not a CTA. It's an identity statement. The user should leave feeling like renting from 9m is an expression of who they are — not just a transaction they completed.

## The Five Emotional Hooks

These are the real feelings the target customer carries. The copy doesn't manufacture them — it names them.

### Hook 1 — The ownership trap
*"I want to document this properly. But I'm not buying a RM 3,000 camera for two trips a year."*

The break-even math is devastating when you say it out loud. Use it. Let the number do the work.

> "A GoPro Hero 13 costs RM 1,800 to own. At RM 45/day, you'd need 40 shoot days a year to break even. Most people shoot 3."

### Hook 2 — The fear of damage
*"What if I scratch it? What if something goes wrong? I'll be too careful to actually use it."*

This is the silent conversion killer. Name the fear and dissolve it — not by explaining the damage policy, but by giving permission.

> "Use it fully. That's what it's for."

### Hook 3 — The documentation urgency
*"This trip is happening once. I want footage I'll actually watch in 10 years."*

The moment is fleeting. The gear makes it permanent. Make the user feel the weight of the undocumented moment — not as guilt, but as recognition. *You already know this.*

### Hook 4 — The identity signal
*"I'm the kind of person who does things properly. Who doesn't half-ass the documentation of something worth doing."*

Renting from 9m is a statement about who you are. You take your adventures seriously. You don't settle for shaky phone footage of a moment that deserved better.

### Hook 5 — The broken market relief
*"Every other option felt sketchy. This feels like it was built by someone who actually cares."*

Don't say "we're better than the competition." Just feel like it was made by people who've been on the other side of a bad rental experience. Specificity and honesty do this better than any claim.

## Terminology

### Use these words

| Word | Not this |
|------|----------|
| **Kit** | package, bundle, set |
| **Shoot** | capture, record, film |
| **Adventure** | experience, activity, outing |
| **Moment** | experience, memory, occasion |
| **Gear** | equipment, devices, items |
| **Book** | order, purchase, rent (as a verb) |
| **Refundable deposit** | deposit (always include "refundable") |
| **Return** | give back, hand back |

### Never use these words

- Seamless, hassle-free, frictionless (show it, don't say it)
- Premium (implies price, not value)
- Solution (corporate filler)
- Journey (unless it's a literal trip)
- Passionate (everyone says this)
- Transparent (show it, don't say it)
- Leverage (never)

### Numbers and pricing

- Always RM in copy (MYR is fine in metadata/schema)
- "From RM 40/day" — the "from" matters, don't drop it
- Deposit: always state it's refundable in the same breath
- Break-even math is a powerful tool — use it sparingly, only when it lands hard

### Malaysian context

- Klang Valley, not just KL (more accurate, more local)
- Mention specific places when relevant — Tioman, Gunung Ledang, Sepang — specificity builds credibility
- FPX, Touch 'n Go — use the actual names, not "local payment methods"
- Don't over-localise. The brand is Malaysian but the voice is global-quality.

## SEO Integration

Good brand copy and good SEO copy are the same thing: answer the question the user is already asking.

### Primary keywords (high intent)
- `action camera rental KL` / `action camera rental Kuala Lumpur`
- `GoPro rental Malaysia` / `GoPro rental KL`
- `Insta360 rental Malaysia`

### Secondary keywords (mid-funnel)
- `action camera rental price Malaysia`
- `underwater camera rental Malaysia`
- `adventure gear rental Klang Valley`

### Long-tail / FAQ keywords (high conversion)
- `how much does it cost to rent a GoPro in KL`
- `GoPro rental deposit Malaysia`
- `can I rent a GoPro for one day Malaysia`
- `action camera rental for diving Malaysia`

### Integration rules

- Primary keywords: H1, meta title, meta description, first 100 words of body copy, FAQ questions
- Secondary keywords: H2s, section headers, gear labels
- Long-tail: FAQ answers — phrase the question exactly as someone would type it into Google
- Never stuff. One awkward keyword does more damage than no keyword.
- The FAQ section is the highest-leverage SEO real estate on the page.

### Meta copy rules

- Title: primary keyword → differentiator → brand. Under 60 characters.
- Description: answer "what is this and why should I care" in one sentence. Include a price anchor. Under 155 characters.
- Current title is correct: `9mrentals — Action Camera Rentals in KL | GoPro, Insta360, DJI`

## The Lines That Should Never Change

These are load-bearing. They define the brand on the page. Don't rewrite them without a very good reason.

1. **"All the gear. Zero ownership."** — the brand in five words. Do not change without a very good reason.
2. **"If not now, when?"** — the brand's core question. Belongs in the closing section.
3. **"Pay for the days you shoot. Not the shelf."** — the best rational argument on the page.
4. **"Use it fully. That's what it's for."** — the damage permission line. Must exist somewhere on the page.

---

## The Copy Quality Test

Before publishing any copy, run it through this filter:

1. **Poster test** — could this line stand alone on a billboard? If not, is it earning its place?
2. **Friend test** — would a real person say this to another real person? If it sounds like a press release, rewrite it.
3. **Cut test** — remove the last word. Does the sentence still work? If yes, cut it. Keep cutting until it breaks.
4. **"So what" test** — after every claim, ask "so what?" If the answer isn't obvious, the claim isn't landing.
5. **Identity test** — does this copy make the reader feel like a specific kind of person? If it could apply to anyone, it applies to no one.

## Section-by-Section Direction

Quick reference for each homepage section. Full rationale in `research/2026-05-copy-strategy.md`.

### Hero
- Headline: keep the tension structure (imperative + negation). Two short lines. The second reframes or challenges.
- Sub-copy: one line that names the feeling, one line that names the price. Feeling first.
- CTA: action verb + specific destination. Implies the adventure is already in motion.
- Eyebrow: "Action camera rentals · Kuala Lumpur" — correct, don't change it.

### Gear Section
- Header: "Gear for what you're shooting." — keep it.
- Kit taglines: short, evocative, activity-specific. "Below the surface." not "Everything you need for a dive trip."
- Kit descriptions: specificity over summary. "Waterproof to 60m. Captures colour at depth." not "Great for diving."
- CTA: "Book this kit →" when dates are set. "Add full kit →" when not.

### Why Us
- Lead with the fear of damage — it's the most emotionally resonant objection.
- The break-even math is the strongest rational argument — make the numbers land hard.
- Section header should name the tension, not describe the section.
- Reorder: damage permission → break-even math → latest model → transparent pricing.

### How It Works
- This section is working. Don't over-write it.
- Fix Step 03: "GrabPay" → "Touch 'n Go" (per payment decisions).
- Footer note ("Deposit is refundable. Damage waiver is optional. No hidden fees.") — keep it.

### Social Proof
- Review copy: specific, activity-referenced, honest-sounding. Keep this register for real reviews.
- Context tags ("Diving · 3 days") — keep this pattern.
- Section H2: consider "Real trips. Real gear. Real people." over a stat-only header.

### FAQ
- Questions: phrased exactly as someone would type them into Google.
- Answers: direct answer in the first sentence, then expand. Never bury the answer.
- Delivery question: rewrite — delivery is not in MVP. "Not yet. Self-collect from [location] at launch. Delivery is coming."
- Deposit answer: 25% of kit MSRP, not individual item retail price.

### Brand Statement (Close)
- Headline: about the person, not the gear. The gear is the means. The moment is the point.
- Sub-copy: this is where "If not now, when?" belongs. Short. Charged. Unanswered.
- CTA 1 (primary): "Pick your kit →" — keep it.
- CTA 2 (secondary): must go to `/store` or booking flow, not back to the gear section.
