## Research: What Makes a Great Pitch Narrative

Sources: Viking Capitalist, 50Proof, Inkppt, Finmodels Lab

### The core principle
A pitch deck is not a fact sheet. It's a story with a narrative arc. Investors are conditioned to say no — the job of the narrative is to move them from skepticism to emotional investment *before* you show them the numbers.

The arc that works:
> **Inciting incident → tension builds → hero emerges → opportunity revealed → proof → ask**

Or in pitch terms:
> **Who we are → where we come from → the problem → the real pain → the gap → the solution → market → competition → why us → business model → vision → ask**

### What makes each section land

- **Opening (Who We Are):** Investors back people first. Lead with founders — not credentials, but *why you*. The personal story creates emotional buy-in before a single business slide appears.
- **Problem:** Don't just state it — dramatize it. Make it tangible, urgent, relatable. The problem is the inciting incident.
- **Pain (real customer pain):** This is where most decks skip a beat. Going from "the problem exists" to "here's our solution" misses the emotional middle — *how does this actually feel for the customer?* This is where empathy lives.
- **Market / Gap:** After pain, the HMW question lands perfectly here. It reframes the problem as an opportunity and sets up the solution.
- **Solution:** Should feel *inevitable* after the problem and pain slides. Not "here's what we built" but "here's what the world was waiting for."
- **Competition:** Don't hide from it. Show you've done the homework. Frame gaps as your opportunity.
- **Business Model:** Should feel like the logical extension of the solution, not a separate financial exercise.
- **Team:** By this point, investors should be nodding. The team slide is the "of course" moment — these are exactly the people who should be solving this.
- **Vision:** Not a 5-year plan. A picture of the world you're building toward. Emotional, not operational.
- **Ask:** Clear, specific, tied to milestones. Not "we need money" but "here's exactly what this capital unlocks."

## Analysis: What the Current Pitch (v1) Gets Right

- Problem statement is sharp and specific — "WhatsApp catalogs, Google Forms, 50% deposits" is vivid
- Solution is clear and well-differentiated
- Business model is solid and grounded
- Vision has a strong anchor ("Decathlon for rentals", Red Bull comparison)

## What's Missing or Needs Improving

1. **No founder story.** The current pitch starts with the problem. There's no "who we are" — no reason to care about *these two people* solving *this problem*. This is the biggest gap.

2. **No emotional middle.** It jumps from "the market is broken" to "here's our solution." The customer's emotional experience — the frustration, the fear, the disappointment — is absent. The Bali incident and the RM 500 scratch story belong here.

3. **No HMW / pivot moment.** There's no single sentence that crystallises the opportunity before the solution is revealed. The HMW question we developed is perfect for this.

4. **Market size is implied, not stated.** The pitch mentions segments but never quantifies the total addressable market. Investors need a number.

5. **Competitors section is thin.** "WhatsApp chaos" is a good frame but doesn't show competitive analysis. Need named competitors, their gaps, and why we win.

6. **The "It's Not Just Cameras" angle is missing.** The expansion opportunity beyond action cameras (lifestyle gear, studio, etc.) is mentioned in vision but not framed as a market insight. This is a missed opportunity to show the bigger picture early.

7. **The Ask is a placeholder.** "Update this section" is not an ask. Needs to be specific: amount, use of funds, milestones it unlocks.

8. **Team slide doesn't exist.** Nadree and Ian are never introduced as people. Their story, their complementary skills, their *why* — all absent.

## Narrative Flow: Proposed Structure

The narrative follows a classic hero's journey adapted for a pitch:

```
ACT 1 — THE WORLD AS IT IS
  1. Who We Are         → establish the founders, create emotional buy-in
  2. Where We Come From → the personal origin story, the friction point
  3. The Problem        → the market is broken (external, observable)
  4. The Real Pain      → how it actually feels (internal, emotional)
  5. It's Not Just Cameras → the problem is bigger than it looks

ACT 2 — THE TURNING POINT
  6. The Gap (HMW)      → reframe: this is an opportunity, not just a problem

ACT 3 — THE WORLD AS IT COULD BE
  7. The Solution       → what we're building
  8. Who We Serve       → the customer, clearly defined
  9. The Market         → how big is this opportunity
  10. Competitors       → what exists, what's missing
  11. What We Learned   → gaps = our opportunities
  12. Our UVP           → why we win

ACT 4 — THE BUSINESS
  13. Business Model    → how we make money
  14. Traction / Where We Are → where we are now

ACT 5 — THE FUTURE
  15. Vision            → where this goes
  16. The Ask           → what we need, what it unlocks
```

**Objective:** The inevitable answer. Should feel like "of course."
**Core message:** A professional, modern and friction-free customer experience at its core — activity-ready kits, a seamless platform, an adventure-aware policy. Everything the market was missing, built for the way people actually adventure.

We're building [BRAND NAME] — the modern adventure gear rental platform for Malaysia.

Not a WhatsApp catalog. Not a Google Form. A real platform.

**Activity-ready kits, not just equipment.**
A diving kit isn't just a camera. It's the housing, the mounts, the batteries, the memory card — everything configured and ready to go. No "oh shit I forgot X" moments.

**A seamless digital experience.**
Real-time availability. Self-service booking. Professional payments — cards, FPX, Touch 'n Go, PayLater. Customer accounts. No back-and-forth.

**A rental structure that makes sense.**
You pay for the days you use the gear — not the days you're coordinating logistics around a trip. Pickup and dropoff windows are separate from your usage dates.

**Adventure-aware damage policy.**
We know adventurers push gear. Minor wear is expected. Use it fully, without fear. We're not here to penalise you for living.

**Lower deposits.**
25–30% at launch (vs. the market's 50%). Transitioning to 10–20% with insurance backing via multiple methods (self-insured with waiver, backed by real insurance companies). Lower barrier, better experience, still protected.
